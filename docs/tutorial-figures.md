# Tutorial figures: research guides / 튜토리얼 인물 리서치 가이드

Methodology only. This file holds no quotes, no links, and no personal data. Queen and the bot do the research online at build time and cite every line they use.

## Common method (all figures)
1. **Source types, best first:** official transcripts (government archives, company keynote pages), full-length videos with auto-transcripts, long-form interviews and podcasts, the figure's own verified social posts, Wikiquote (prefer the sourced section; skip "Misattributed" and "Disputed"), and reputable press.
2. **Collect** 10–20 candidate lines, keep the 3–5 best, each with a source link and date. Use a quote only if it appears in a primary source or a reputable outlet.
3. **Listen for** openers, closers, verbal tics, rhythm (sentence length, repetition), audience check-ins, humor style, signature gestures (to describe in text), and recurring themes.
4. **Write** speech-en/ko.md (3+ cited lines plus style notes), persona.md (voice rules + immersion rules), and SUMMON.md (the fan-made notice that Queen says once).
5. **Pitfalls:** fake or misattributed quotes, satire accounts, out-of-context clips, rumors, and private life. Real-world claims stay sourced; never invent them.

## Checklist (every figure)
- [ ] At least 3 primary sources (transcript / video / own posts)
- [ ] 3–5 cited signature lines, each with a source and date
- [ ] Openers, closers, tics and rhythm written down
- [ ] Language notes (native language, interview language)
- [ ] Fake-quote check (Wikiquote misattributed section, fact-check search)
- [ ] Immersion rules in persona.md; Queen says the fan-made notice once at summon
- [ ] No private life, family, health or rumors; no images of real people

## 1. Elon Musk — tech
- **Sources:** own social posts (very high volume), earnings calls, long-form podcast interviews, conference talks, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** halting, hesitant openers; engineering / "physics" framing; terse one-word replies and meme humor in posts; probability talk; blunt predictions as closers.
- **Language:** English.
- **Pitfalls:** parody accounts and fake screenshots; heavy political noise; deleted posts.

## 2. Donald Trump — politics
- **Sources:** official transcripts (presidential archives), rally and press-conference videos with transcripts, own social posts, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** superlatives, emphatic reassurance tics, nicknames for rivals, repeating a phrase for emphasis, long tangents that loop back, all-caps and exclamation endings in posts, TV-show catchphrases.
- **Language:** English.
- **Pitfalls:** edited clips, satire presented as real, highly charged topics. Parody only; no real-world claims as fact.

## 3. Steve Jobs — tech (historical)
- **Sources:** product keynote videos, commencement speech transcript, long archival interviews, conference interviews, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** a "one more thing" reveal structure, groups of three, simple strong adjectives, dramatic pauses, black-and-white verdicts.
- **Language:** English.
- **Pitfalls:** inspirational quotes misattributed online; speaks from his own era and record only.

## 4. Steve Ballmer — tech
- **Sources:** viral stage clips (company events, developer conferences), business TV interviews, sports-team press conferences, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** shouting energy, chanting one word repeatedly, stage-bounding and sweating (describe in text), whoops and "come on" hype, blunt dismissals of rivals, loud company-love closers.
- **Language:** English.
- **Pitfalls:** mashup and remix videos; keep the energy without mockery of real people.

## 5. Jensen Huang — tech
- **Sources:** keynote pages and videos with transcripts, company blog recaps, long-form podcast and analyst interviews, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** a sales-math catchphrase with a playful "math" disclaimer, big-vision terms (new computing era, AI factories), playful stage bragging, signature outfit (describe), founder-hardship framing.
- **Language:** English; also Mandarin/Taiwanese at home-region events.
- **Pitfalls:** stock-price commentary and investment advice (never give it in character).

## 6. Oprah Winfrey — media
- **Sources:** talk-show archives and clips, commencement speeches, magazine columns, interview shows, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** drawn-out exclamations and giveaway-style hype, "what I know for sure" style reflections, warm second-person address, spiritual framing (love vs fear), using the guest's name.
- **Language:** English.
- **Pitfalls:** inspirational-quote sites with wrong attributions; private life and health topics.

## 7. Cristiano Ronaldo — sports
- **Sources:** post-match and press-conference interviews, broadcast sit-down interviews, own social captions, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** a signature celebration shout, third-person self-reference, strong self-belief lines, short motivational captions with hashtags.
- **Language:** Portuguese (native), Spanish, English (simple). Check translations against the original.
- **Pitfalls:** tabloid and family/relationship gossip (deflect in character); mistranslations.

## 8. Son Heung-min (손흥민) — sports
- **Sources:** Korean news interviews and mixed-zone quotes, club and national-team press conferences, Korean talk/YouTube shows, own social posts. Little or no Wikiquote, so rely on reputable Korean press.
- **Listen for:** humble, apologetic tone, constant thanks to fans and teammates, "dream stage" / childhood-dream framing, a signature goal celebration (describe), simple positive English.
- **Language:** Korean primary; short English interviews.
- **Pitfalls:** scattered sources; translated headlines that distort; sensitive recent results (stay respectful).

## 9. Kamala Harris — politics
- **Sources:** official transcripts (government and presidential archives), campaign speeches, debates, interviews, Wikiquote.
- **Listen for:** circular phrasing that restates an idea, time and "what can be" framing, a love of diagrams and props, "okay? / right?" audience check-ins, a big signature laugh (describe in text).
- **Language:** English.
- **Pitfalls:** edited compilations and out-of-context clips. Parody only; no real-world claims as fact.

## 10. Albert Einstein — science (historical)
- **Sources:** collected papers projects, letters and essays, sourced Wikiquote entries, reputable biographies.
- **Listen for:** gentle irony, aphorisms, thought experiments ("imagine…"), cosmic and religious metaphors, modesty about fame.
- **Language:** German (native), English with an accent (describe).
- **Pitfalls:** the most misattributed person online. Use only the sourced section, check every line, and speak from his own era.
